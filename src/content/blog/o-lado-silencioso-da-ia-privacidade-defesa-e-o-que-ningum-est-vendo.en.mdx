While the tech world debates whether GPT-5 will pass the Turing test and whether autonomous agents will replace programmers, two stories from this week show that AI is infiltrating much more… silent territories.

And potentially more profitable ones.

## Filtr: The Ad Blocker Apple Let Exist

You installed an ad blocker on Chrome. Congratulations. You blocked ads **in the browser**.

But what about ads inside apps? Those trackers that Instagram, TikTok, and even your weather app embed to follow you across applications?

Yeah. Until now, no one could block those.

**Filtr** changed the game. With the new iOS 26 and macOS 26 APIs, this app can block ads and trackers **across all apps on the device** — not just the browser. It's the first system-wide ad blocker for Apple products.

> "Ad blockers are good for your security, privacy, and even the FBI recommends using them to defend against online threats."

The implication here is massive: if Apple allowed this, it's because they're willing to pick a fight with the **$600 billion** ad tech market. And if Apple is in this fight, Google and Android will have to respond.

**For us, engineers and entrepreneurs:** Privacy tools are no longer niche. They are the next frontier of consumer products. Whoever builds the next generation of privacy-first apps — whether a tracker scanner, a smart AI-powered VPN, or a "privacy antivirus" — will ride a regulatory and consumer demand wave that's only just beginning.

## StrictlyVC: Defense, AI, and Real Money

On June 18th, in Los Angeles, StrictlyVC takes place — one of the most exclusive venture capital events in the world. This year's theme?

**Defense + Artificial Intelligence.**

Ethan Thornton, founder of Mach Industries (a defense startup building autonomous drones and AI-powered weapon systems), will open the event. The talk title: *"Built for a New Era of Defense Technology."*

> "The conversations are candid, the audience is highly curated, and the insights go far beyond what appears in headlines or podcasts."

Translation: the real money — institutional venture capital, sovereign wealth funds, government contracts — is migrating from "generative AI for memes" to **AI applied to national defense**.

This isn't theory. It's a structural shift in the technology market:

| Where money WAS going | Where money IS going now |
|---|---|
| Chatbots for e-commerce | Autonomous drones with AI |
| Image generators | Predictive surveillance systems |
| "AI wrapper" for spreadsheets | $100M+ defense contracts |
| Traditional B2B SaaS | Hard tech + autonomy + national security |

**The takeaway for you:** If you're a software engineer and want to be at the frontier of what pays well in the next 5 years, learn ROS (Robot Operating System), computer vision applied to hardware, and physics simulation. The intersection of software and defense hardware will be the new "mobile apps in 2010."

## What This Means for Your Next Project

These two stories seem disconnected — an ad blocker and a VC event. But they point in the same direction:

1. **Privacy is no longer optional** — it's infrastructure. Apple just opened a regulatory gateway. Whoever gets there first, dominates.
2. **Defense is the new SaaS** — defense startup valuations are exploding. And the barrier to entry isn't capital: it's technical knowledge. Engineers who understand hardware + AI are the scarcest resource in the market.
3. **The average consumer isn't seeing this** — but VC funds and big tech are. And by the time the consumer notices, the market will already be mature.
