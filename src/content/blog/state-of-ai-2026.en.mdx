## The AI Landscape Today

2026 is the year AI goes from experimental to essential. Here's what's changed and what matters for engineers.

## Models Are Commoditizing

The gap between GPT-4, Claude, and open-source models is shrinking fast. What used to be a competitive moat is now the baseline.

**What this means for you:**
- Don't build your product around a specific model
- Abstract your LLM calls behind a provider interface
- Invest in prompt engineering, not model selection

## The Rise of Agentic Workflows

Tools like LangChain, CrewAI, and AutoGen have matured. But the real innovation is in **orchestration** — how agents coordinate, delegate, and recover from errors.

## Tools That Actually Matter

Here's what I'm using day to day:

- **Cursor / Windsurf** — AI-native IDEs that actually improve productivity
- **Vercel AI SDK** — Streaming, tool calls, and structured outputs ready to use
- **LangSmith / Weave** — Observability for LLM calls
- **Supabase / Neon** — Serverless databases with vector search

## What to Ignore

- **Hype cycles** — AGI isn't arriving next month
- **"No-code AI"** — If you're an engineer, learn the fundamentals
- **Single-shot agent demos** — Production is 100x harder than a demo

## What to Learn

1. **Prompt engineering** — It's a real skill, not a passing fad
2. **Evaluation frameworks** — How do you know if your AI is good?
3. **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** — Still the most practical AI pattern
4. **Streaming and real-time UX** — Users expect instant responses

## The Bottom Line

AI isn't magic. It's software. Build it like software — with testing, monitoring, and iteration. The engineers who treat AI as just another tool in their toolkit will be the ones delivering great products.
