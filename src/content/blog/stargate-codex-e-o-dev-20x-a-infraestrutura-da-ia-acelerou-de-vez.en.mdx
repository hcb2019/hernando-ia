While yesterday's headlines were obsessed with Anthropic's trillion-dollar IPO and Brian Chesky's new AI lab, three stories flew under the radar — and they're the ones that actually matter if you build software.

This isn't about valuation. It's about infrastructure, development velocity, and what happens when the two converge.

## Stargate: 1 Gigawatt of Ambition

OpenAI broke ground in Michigan. Literally. Construction has begun on Project Stargate — a **1 gigawatt data center** dedicated entirely to training and serving AI models.

To put that in perspective: 1 GW is roughly the output of a standard nuclear power plant. It's enough electricity to power about 700,000 American homes. And it's all going to GPUs.

This isn't hyperbole: **AI infrastructure is reaching utility scale.** Data centers aren't those climate-controlled warehouses full of racked servers anymore. They're computation plants, with energy, thermal, and logistical demands comparable to the largest civil engineering projects on Earth.

Project Stargate isn't just a data center. It's OpenAI's bet that inference demand is about to explode — and that whoever controls the infrastructure controls the game. Think AWS in 2008. Now multiply by a thousand.

## Wasmer: From 6 Months to 3 Weeks with Codex

On the other end of the spectrum — not in physical scale, but in day-to-day impact for developers — Wasmer shared how they used **Codex with GPT-5.5** to build an **edge-optimized Node.js runtime**.

The number that makes any tech lead blink: **10x to 20x faster than traditional development cycles.** What normally takes months — understanding specs, writing bindings, debugging memory leaks, iterating — was done in weeks.

But the key detail isn't the speed. It's the *kind of work* Codex handled. This wasn't CRUD boilerplate generation. It was:

- Analyzing and porting V8 runtime APIs
- Optimizing execution paths for WebAssembly
- Identifying and fixing performance bottlenecks
- Generating integration tests for edge scenarios

This isn't "autocomplete on steroids." This is a pair programmer that understands systems.

## Endava: AI Agents on the Production Line

While Wasmer shows what a small, focused team can accomplish with Codex, Endava — a global technology consultancy with thousands of engineers — shows the enterprise-scale picture.

The company redesigned its software delivery pipeline around **AI agents** running on ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex. Not about replacing developers. About automating what should never have been manual: code review, documentation generation, bug triage, microservice scaffolding.

The result, per Endava's account published on OpenAI's blog: shorter delivery cycles, less rework, and — perhaps most importantly — senior engineers spending more time on architecture and less on mechanical tasks.

## What Connects These Three Dots?

**Infrastructure + Tools + Process.** That's the tripod of the new software development era.

Stargate solves the physical layer: no power, no GPU, no inference. Codex solves the tool layer: implementation speeds that were unthinkable 12 months ago. And Endava's agents solve the process layer: how to orchestrate human and non-human teams within a real production pipeline.

None of these pieces work in isolation. But together, they tell a story that goes far beyond "AI is coming for your job":

**The barrier to building quality software is collapsing.** Not because writing code got easier — but because the bottleneck shifted. Before, the limit was typing speed and the ability to hold mental state across complex systems. Now, the limit is specification quality and architectural clarity.

In other words: the developer of the future won't be measured by lines of code per day. They'll be measured by the quality of decisions made while Codex executes.

And for that, we're going to need a lot of power. Literally.
